How common is common sense?

People around the world tell us whether they agree with short statements, and whether they think most other people would. From those two answers we measure how widely a belief is shared, and whether people know it is.

03 · The scores

A score for every statement, and for every person

We count something as common sense when people agree about it and know that others agree. Both scores run from 0 to 100%, and both need both parts to be high.

Statement Scores How commonsensical is a statement? High when nearly everyone gives the same answer and expects others to give it too. Open Statement Scores → Individual Scores How commonsensical is a person? High when someone's own answers side with the majority and they can tell what the majority thinks. Open Individual Scores →

Compare two countries in Group Comparison, or see each statement country by country in Countries.

04 · Design points

What kinds of knowledge are common sense?

Every statement is tagged with six features, which gives 26 = 64 kinds of statement, or design points. Comparing scores across them shows which kinds of knowledge people share most, and so what human common sense is made of.

Feature Gap

Average statement score on each side of a feature. Features overlap, and are also tied to where and when statements were collected, so read each gap as a description, not a cause.
